METEOROLOGICAL BALLOON ASCENTS

What is a radiosonde?

A radiosonde is a lightweight instrument carried into the atmosphere by a weather balloon. During ascent, it measures the state of the atmosphere and transmits observations to a ground receiving system.

From these measurements, meteorologists build a vertical profile of temperature, humidity, pressure and wind — essential information for weather analysis, forecasting and atmospheric research.

WHY UPPER-AIR DATA MATTERS

One ascent. A vertical picture of the atmosphere.

Radiosonde observations support meteorological operations wherever the vertical structure of the atmosphere matters.

01

Weather forecasting

Atmospheric profiles provide observations used in synoptic analysis and numerical weather prediction.

02

Aviation meteorology

Wind, temperature and stability profiles help characterize conditions through the troposphere and lower stratosphere.

03

Atmospheric research

High-resolution vertical observations support field campaigns, validation studies and atmospheric science.

04

Environmental monitoring

Soundings help describe atmospheric transport, boundary-layer structure and dispersion conditions.
